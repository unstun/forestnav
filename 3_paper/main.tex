\begin{abstract}
Hybrid A* remains a practical baseline for non-holonomic vehicle planning because it searches directly in a kinematic state space and can return collision-free, curvature-constrained paths. Its cost, however, grows in dense forest maps where narrow gaps make long-range analytic expansion unreliable. This paper studies whether the planning problem can be decomposed into short, Reeds--Shepp-verifiable subproblems without replacing the planner's feasibility machinery. We introduce F-N3P, an oracle-supervised subgoal decomposition method that converts successful Hybrid A* teacher paths into body-frame $SE(2)$ subgoal labels and retrieves subgoals online with a 41-dimensional map feature and a k-d tree K-nearest-neighbor library. In a formal procedural-forest evaluation with six methods, three validation difficulty buckets, five random seeds, and 1,800 method-query records, F-N3P reduced median planning time by 92.6\% in the Complex bucket and 89.8\% in the Extreme bucket relative to vanilla Hybrid A*, while improving feasible rate and maintaining zero collision violations. Framework-scale ablations support the use of multi-neighbor retrieval and the fallback hierarchy. Framework-scale generalization tests show promising out-of-distribution procedural-density behavior but do not yet establish RealMap transfer; the RealMap aggregate failed the pre-registered 20\% time-benefit criterion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Autonomous navigation in dense forests is a difficult case for non-holonomic path planning. The vehicle must respect an Ackermann steering model, avoid full-body collisions with tree trunks or mapped obstacles, and often move through narrow passages where small heading changes determine whether a candidate path is feasible. Hybrid A* is attractive in this setting because it searches in a three-dimensional kinematic state space and uses analytic expansions to close feasible segments when possible \cite{Dolgov2010HybridAstar}. The same mechanism also creates a computational bottleneck. In cluttered maps, a single long global search may spend much of its budget exploring states that would be unnecessary if the planner were given a small number of good intermediate $SE(2)$ targets.

Learning-based motion planning offers one route to reduce this search burden, but replacing a geometric planner with a learned policy weakens the direct connection to collision checking and kinematic feasibility \cite{Wang2021LearningMotionPlanning}. The design goal in this paper is therefore narrower. F-N3P does not learn a complete planner. It learns a subgoal proposal mechanism and keeps Reeds--Shepp collision checking, bounded local Hybrid A*, and full vanilla Hybrid A* fallback in the loop. This choice follows the spirit of N3P, where a learned module predicts a preparatory pose and Hybrid A* handles the feasible connection \cite{Xue2026N3P}, but it changes the structure from a single parking preparatory pose to a sequential subgoal decomposition for long forest queries.

The central question is whether oracle-supervised subgoals can make dense-forest Hybrid A* planning faster without losing the feasibility properties that make the original planner useful. F-N3P answers this by collecting successful teacher paths from vanilla Hybrid A*, segmenting them with a greedy Reeds--Shepp-reachable rule, and storing the resulting local geometric decisions in a K-nearest-neighbor library. At test time, the planner repeatedly retrieves candidate body-frame $SE(2)$ subgoals from local map features, verifies them with the same collision checker used by the baseline, and falls back to local or full Hybrid A* when retrieval fails.

The contribution of this draft is a complete experimental scaffold for evaluating this decomposition idea in procedural forests and early real-map transfer tests. Under the current formal T14 procedural-map evaluation, F-N3P passed the pre-registered Complex and Extreme gates: it reduced median planning time by more than 50\%, did not reduce feasible rate, kept median path inflation below 5\%, and produced zero collision violations. The current evidence is not yet a final real-map generalization claim. The T16 RealMap aggregate failed the pre-registered 20\% time-benefit criterion at framework scale, and the paper keeps that boundary explicit.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Hybrid A* and bounded-curvature vehicle planning.}
Hybrid A* is a standard planning approach for car-like robots because it combines grid search with continuous pose states and analytic expansion, allowing paths to respect non-holonomic vehicle constraints while still using search-based planning structure \cite{Dolgov2010HybridAstar,Sormoli2024HybridSurvey}. Reeds--Shepp curves provide the bounded-curvature forward-and-reverse local connection model used by many car-like planners, including the analytic connection and verification logic in this work \cite{ReedsShepp1990}. F-N3P keeps this geometric core unchanged. Its learning component proposes intermediate $SE(2)$ subgoals, but every committed segment is still checked by the same Reeds--Shepp and Hybrid A* machinery used by the baseline.

\paragraph{Roadmaps, waypoints, and subgoal structure.}
Roadmap methods reduce planning cost by building graph structure in configuration space, with probabilistic roadmaps being a foundational example \cite{Kavraki1996PRM}. More recent vehicle-planning work also combines Hybrid A* with roadmap or waypoint structure in narrow industrial environments \cite{Bonetti2023Roadmap}. These methods motivate a useful comparison for forests: if a hand-built geometric decomposition is enough, learning is unnecessary. The baselines in this paper therefore include Voronoi and bottleneck subgoal planners. F-N3P differs from these baselines by learning the next $SE(2)$ subgoal from teacher trajectories rather than deriving all intermediate targets from a static roadmap, medial axis, or bottleneck rule.

\paragraph{Learning-assisted motion planning.}
Learning-based motion planning has been studied as a way to guide sampling, predict costs, or directly generate planning actions \cite{Wang2021LearningMotionPlanning}. For safety-critical vehicle planning, however, the main risk is not only prediction error but also the loss of a clear feasibility certificate. F-N3P takes a conservative position in this design space. The learned module is a proposal mechanism over local subgoals, not an authority that can bypass collision checking. Failed predictions are visible through fallback metrics, and full Hybrid A* remains the terminal recovery planner.

\paragraph{From N3P parking to forest subgoal decomposition.}
N3P shows that a learned preparatory pose can accelerate automated parking by handing a better intermediate target to Hybrid A* \cite{Xue2026N3P}. F-N3P reuses this division of labor but changes the problem geometry. Parking is a short structured maneuver, whereas forest navigation requires repeated decisions through unstructured obstacle fields. The method therefore turns one preparatory-pose prediction into sequential subgoal prediction, replaces parking-scene features with a ray-cast clearance profile and local density features from a prior occupancy grid map, and evaluates whether this decomposition improves dense-forest planning under explicit fallback and collision-checking gates.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
The task is global path planning for an Ackermann-steered vehicle in a prior occupancy grid map. A query consists of an occupancy grid map, an initial pose, and a goal pose. The planner must return a collision-free path in $SE(2)$ that satisfies the vehicle minimum-turning-radius constraint and the full-body collision model.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}

F-N3P is an oracle-supervised subgoal decomposition method for dense-forest planning. The method follows the main design principle of N3P, which accelerates parking by predicting an intermediate preparatory pose and delegating kinodynamic feasibility to Hybrid A* \cite{Xue2026N3P}. The difference is structural. Parking N3P predicts one preparatory pose for a local parking maneuver, whereas ForestNav must plan through a long, non-structured forest map with multiple narrow passages. F-N3P therefore predicts a sequence of $SE(2)$ subgoals. Each predicted subgoal defines a short local planning problem, and each local problem is solved or verified by the same Hybrid A* and Reeds--Shepp components used by the vanilla planner \cite{Dolgov2010HybridAstar,ReedsShepp1990}.

The pipeline has an offline stage and an online stage. Offline, vanilla Hybrid A* solves many procedural forest queries. Successful teacher paths are converted into oracle labels by a greedy Reeds--Shepp-reachable segmentation rule. Each label pairs a 41-dimensional hand-crafted map feature with the next body-frame subgoal displacement. These pairs form a k-d tree K-nearest-neighbor library. Online, the planner starts from the initial pose, checks whether a direct collision-free Reeds--Shepp connection to the goal exists, and otherwise queries the library for the next subgoal. A fallback hierarchy keeps the method tied to the original planner: failed learned predictions are retried with additional neighbors, then with bounded local Hybrid A*, and finally with full vanilla Hybrid A* on the original query.

\subsection{Map and Vehicle Model}
\label{sec:method_model}

The environment is represented by a prior occupancy grid map $\mathcal{M}: G \rightarrow \{0,1\}$ at resolution $r=0.1$ m, where occupied cells represent tree trunks or mapped obstacles. A vehicle pose is $\mathbf{x}=(x,y,\theta)\in SE(2)$. The vehicle footprint $B(\mathbf{x})$ is represented by a dual-circle approximation of the vehicle body, matching the collision model used in the planner implementation. The collision-free state set is
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathrm{free}} = \{\mathbf{x}\in SE(2): B(\mathbf{x})\cap \mathrm{Occ}(\mathcal{M})=\emptyset\}.
\end{equation}
A feasible path is a continuous sequence of poses in $X_{\mathrm{free}}$ that satisfies the Ackermann steering constraint through a minimum turning radius.

The underlying local connector is a Reeds--Shepp curve. For two poses $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{x}'$, $RS(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')$ denotes the shortest forward-and-reverse bounded-curvature connection in obstacle-free space \cite{ReedsShepp1990}. The predicate $RS_{\mathrm{free}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')$ is true when sampled poses along that curve pass the dual-circle collision detector. The global teacher and local segment planner are Hybrid A* instances with analytic expansion enabled. Hybrid A* searches over the three-dimensional kinematic state space and uses analytic expansions to connect to the goal when possible \cite{Dolgov2010HybridAstar}.

\subsection{Oracle Label Extraction}
\label{sec:method_labels}

The oracle data source is the vanilla Hybrid A* teacher. For a successful teacher path $P:[0,S]\rightarrow X_{\mathrm{free}}$ parameterized by arc length, F-N3P extracts a sequence of subgoals by repeatedly taking the farthest downstream pose that is reachable from the current pose by a collision-free Reeds--Shepp curve of bounded length. Let $g_0=P(0)$ and let $s_i$ be the arc-length index of the current subgoal $g_i$. The candidate set at step $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_i =
\left\{
s\in(s_i,S] :
RS_{\mathrm{free}}(g_i,P(s)) \land
\mathrm{len}(RS(g_i,P(s))) \le L_{\max}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{S}_i$ is empty, the teacher path is discarded for labeling. Otherwise the next subgoal is $g_{i+1}=P(\max \mathcal{S}_i)$. If the arc-length progress $\max \mathcal{S}_i-s_i$ is below $L_{\min}$, the path is also discarded. This prevents very short, fragmented labels in dense regions. The current implementation uses $L_{\max}=8.0$ m. The formal training dataset used $L_{\min}=1.0$ m after the label-pilot failure-rate check.

For every accepted transition, the label is not the global subgoal pose itself. Instead, the target is the body-frame displacement
\begin{equation}
\Delta_i = g_{i+1}\ominus g_i = (\Delta x_i,\Delta y_i,\Delta\theta_i),
\end{equation}
where $\ominus$ denotes the relative $SE(2)$ transform expressed in the frame of $g_i$. This representation removes global translation and rotation from the supervised target. The final teacher segment is handled by the online stopping rule and does not produce an additional label: when the current pose has a collision-free direct Reeds--Shepp connection to the goal, the planner closes the path directly.

\subsection{Forest Environment Abstraction}
\label{sec:method_features}

F-N3P uses a 41-dimensional hand-crafted feature vector $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_g,\mathcal{M})$. All geometric quantities are expressed in the current vehicle frame. The feature design replaces the low-dimensional parking-scene abstraction in N3P with a map query that applies to unstructured tree distributions.

The ray-cast clearance profile is a pure geometric query on the prior occupancy grid map. From the current pose, the implementation casts 32 uniformly spaced rays over $2\pi$ and records the distance to the first occupied cell or map boundary, truncated at $R_{\max}=10$ m. It is computed from the map before planning, does not use online range sensing, and is used only as an environment abstraction for nearest-neighbor retrieval. Before building the nearest-neighbor structure, each feature dimension is z-score normalized using the training-set mean and standard deviation.

\subsection{KNN Subgoal Predictor}
\label{sec:method_knn}

The supervised dataset is $\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{f}_n,\Delta_n)\}_{n=1}^N$. The implementation builds a k-d tree over the normalized feature vectors and stores the corresponding three-dimensional labels. At query time, the predictor returns the $k$ nearest labels. For a current pose $\mathbf{x}$ and a predicted body-frame displacement $\Delta$, the world-frame subgoal is
\begin{equation}
\hat{g} = \mathbf{x}\circ \Delta,
\end{equation}
where $\circ$ is $SE(2)$ composition. The formal main evaluation uses $k=20$ candidate neighbors and commits a Reeds--Shepp segment immediately when the candidate segment has already passed collision checking. The software also exposes masked-feature KNN and an MLP regressor for ablation studies, but the main method is the full-feature KNN predictor.

The current training dataset contains 100,531 samples with 41-dimensional features and three-dimensional labels. It was generated from 2,000 procedural maps and 80,000 planning queries. The teacher success and label extraction process are reported in the T08 experiment ledger; these numbers describe the current implementation state and are not used as result claims in this Method section.

\subsection{Online Planning with Fallback}
\label{sec:method_online}

Given a new query $(\mathcal{M},\mathbf{x}_s,\mathbf{x}_g)$, F-N3P initializes the current pose $\mathbf{p}$ to $\mathbf{x}_s$ and an output path to the singleton path at $\mathbf{x}_s$. At each iteration, it first tests $RS_{\mathrm{free}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x}_g)$. If the test succeeds, the planner appends the Reeds--Shepp curve and returns. Otherwise it extracts $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x}_g,\mathcal{M})$, queries the KNN library, transforms each candidate displacement into a world-frame subgoal, and checks whether that subgoal is collision-free and Reeds--Shepp reachable from the current pose.

The fallback hierarchy has three levels. F1 retries additional nearest-neighbor candidates when the first predicted subgoal is occupied or fails the Reeds--Shepp collision test. F2 runs bounded Hybrid A* either to the predicted subgoal or directly to the goal, with a segment budget smaller than the full teacher budget. F3 abandons the learned decomposition and runs vanilla Hybrid A* on the original query with the same full-query budget used by the baseline. The planner also triggers F3 if the distance to the goal fails to decrease for two consecutive prediction steps or if the adaptive maximum step count is reached. This hierarchy does not claim a stronger completeness property than vanilla Hybrid A*. It gives F-N3P the same budgeted fallback coverage as the baseline while making the cost of failed predictions visible through fallback-rate metrics.

\subsection{Implementation and Evaluation Interface}
\label{sec:method_implementation}

The implementation separates method construction from evaluation. The feature extractor, label extractor, KNN library builder, online inference loop, baselines, and metric writer are separate modules under \texttt{2\_experiment/forest\_n3p}. Evaluation records store success, feasibility, total wall-clock time, node expansions, path length, path inflation relative to vanilla Hybrid A*, direction switches, mean curvature, minimum clearance, collision violations, fallback counts, and subgoal reachability. The same record schema is used for the main evaluation, ablations, and generalization tests. Returned paths are rejected as infeasible if post hoc dual-circle collision checking finds any collision violation.

This separation is important for the paper claims. The Method section defines how F-N3P constructs subgoals and paths. The Results section will only use metrics from the experiment ledgers. In particular, the current T16 generalization run is a framework-scale candidate run, not a final paper-scale RealMap claim. The paper should therefore not state final generalization conclusions until the corresponding Result tables are frozen.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
The current result package contains one formal main evaluation and two framework-scale follow-up runs. The formal main evaluation is the T14 run with six methods, three validation difficulty buckets, five random seeds, 100 queries per bucket, and 1,800 method-query records in total. The ablation and generalization runs are included here because they define the current experimental boundary, but they are not yet used as final paper-scale evidence. All returned paths were rechecked by the same dual-circle collision detector used by the evaluation framework, and all reported collision-violation counts were zero.

\subsection{Formal Main Evaluation}
\label{sec:results_main}

Table~\ref{tab:main_eval} reports the feasible rate and median wall-clock planning time from the formal T14 run. F-N3P had the highest feasible rate among the learned variants and the lowest median planning time in all three validation buckets. The gap widened as the query bucket became harder: in the Extreme bucket, F-N3P reached a 94\% feasible rate with a median planning time of 0.066 s, whereas vanilla Hybrid A* reached a 70\% feasible rate with a median planning time of 0.650 s. The N3P-style $k=1$ variant remained feasible on 80\% of Extreme queries, but its median time was 0.488 s, showing that a single nearest-neighbor prediction did not recover the main method's speed. Voronoi and bottleneck subgoal baselines were robust, but their median times stayed close to the vanilla planner in the harder buckets. The MD-DQN adapter produced almost no feasible paths in this benchmark, so this row should be read as an implementation-level baseline outcome under the current adapter rather than as a general claim about value-based planners.

\subsection{Generalization Framework Run}
\label{sec:results_generalization}

The T16 generalization package is intentionally reported as a framework-scale run. It used 16 queries and 32 method records, with five queries in each out-of-distribution density bucket and six RealMap queries. Table~\ref{tab:generalization_snapshot} shows the current evidence. The out-of-distribution density rows did not trigger the pre-registered failure criterion on success-rate drop. F-N3P matched vanilla Hybrid A* on OOD-Sparse and exceeded it on OOD-Dense in this small run. The RealMap aggregate did not pass the pre-registered time-benefit criterion. Both F-N3P and vanilla Hybrid A* had a 33.3\% feasible rate on RealMap, and F-N3P was slower at the aggregate median. The current paper draft should therefore describe RealMap transfer as unresolved and should not claim final real-map generalization until a larger reviewed run replaces this framework-scale evidence.

Figure~\ref{fig:generalization_boundary} makes the same boundary visible. The RealMap bar is negative because the current F-N3P aggregate median is slower than vanilla Hybrid A*. This figure is included to prevent the procedural-map result from being overread as a real-map transfer claim.

The current evidence supports one strong claim and two bounded claims. The strong claim is that, under the reviewed validation cutpoints and T14 formal scale, F-N3P passed the pre-registered Complex and Extreme procedural-map gates while maintaining zero collision violations. The first bounded claim is that the ablation framework points toward the importance of multi-neighbor KNN retrieval and the fallback hierarchy, but larger ablation runs are still required before ranking all design factors. The second bounded claim is that out-of-distribution procedural densities look promising at framework scale, whereas RealMap transfer remains an open failure boundary under the current evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper develops F-N3P as a conservative learning-assisted extension of Hybrid A* for dense forest planning. The method uses vanilla Hybrid A* teacher paths to learn local $SE(2)$ subgoal decisions, retrieves candidate subgoals from hand-crafted map features, and commits only segments that pass the same collision and Reeds--Shepp feasibility checks used by the baseline. In the formal procedural-map evaluation, this decomposition substantially reduced median planning time in the Complex and Extreme buckets while preserving or improving feasible rate and maintaining zero collision violations.

The current evidence also defines the remaining boundary of the work. The ablation package is still framework-scale and should be expanded before making final design-factor claims. The out-of-distribution procedural-density run is promising at small scale, but the current RealMap aggregate did not meet the pre-registered time-benefit criterion. The next paper-ready step is therefore not to strengthen the claim rhetorically, but to generate figures, complete citation audit, and either replace the framework-scale generalization evidence with larger reviewed runs or report RealMap transfer as an explicit limitation.
